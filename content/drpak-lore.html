After Weaver's first change, the Garden was different - like nature before a storm, it had grown quieter. That is what happens when structure admits a law that cannot be bypassed.

Pak did not believe in laws he could not cut and test. He approached the altered Thread - the one from which Thirst had been removed but returned. His fingers entered the light precisely, without excess motion, then froze - there was nothing left to cut. Before, Thirst had looked like a tumor, something foreign, but now it had fused into the fibers, become part of the weave. A man on Earth had taken a step, cried out his no, and reality had fixed that pattern forever.

Pak frowned.

- Impossible.

- It has already happened, Dream said quietly. - We were too late here. This Thread's pattern can no longer be undone.

For the first time Pak felt irritation without a clean edge.

- Then the next cut must be made there, he said. - Before she reaches the others, Pak said, scanning the Threads around him.

Noticing one in which a Shadow of Thirst was thickening, he smiled with satisfaction.

- Pak, stop! Dream exclaimed. - We need to discuss everything!

- No, Dream. The cut must be there, in the human world.

And he went into the human world.

He found the woman whose Thread held a Shadow of Thirst in a hospital corridor. White light, the smell of antiseptic and cheap coffee. On her Thread in the Garden a dangerous shift was already ripening, and in the Evil Forest the Shadow tossed - Weaver was about to find its reflection.

The woman sat on a plastic chair, staring blankly at her phone screen. On the screen glowed a typed message: "I can't take it anymore. I'm leaving." She was ready to detonate. Burn everything to hell, destroy her life, just to stop enduring.

Pak stood behind her. Before, he would simply have cut Thirst out in the Garden. Now that was useless: one person, one act. If Weaver pushed her hand first, the Thread would change beyond recall. Pak had to fix the form before the explosion.

He leaned to her ear. His voice sounded cold as a scalpel.

- You do not want freedom. You want a fire.

The woman flinched, looked back, and even screamed at the sight of Pak's giant figure in a white coat. Her fingers froze above the send button.

- Who are YOU?..

- If you burn everything now, Pak continued evenly, cooling her panic, - you will not become yourself. You will become debris. You want to stop being convenient? That is reasonable; I accept such a decision. But make the cut cleanly.

The woman breathed heavily, hoarsely. She shook. The Shadow of Thirst pulled her to press send, demanding immediate revolt. But Pak's cold voice held the edge, keeping her from falling into the abyss. He forced Thirst to grow a spine.

- Do not surrender, Pak ordered. - But do not explode either. Write: "I will no longer pretend I agree. Tomorrow we will talk."

The woman's fingers trembled. For a second she wavered between a wild cry and harsh honesty. Then she erased the old text. Typed new words. And pressed send.

In that same second in the Garden of Threads her Thread flared sharply and set like stone. Golden light fixed the form beyond undoing. From the Evil Forest came a dull, disappointed crack of branches - Weaver had run to this woman's Shadow, but the reflection had already closed. The choice was made. End of the game.

Pak returned to the Garden an instant later. His face was calm, but his eyes had darkened.

- Thirst remained, Dream stated in surprise, looking at the glowing Thread, - but I have never seen such a form… It is part of the Thread now, as with Weaver. But you made it so the Thread still grows along its Path.

- Yes, Pak answered. - Now the Thread will not tear out of its form.

He looked at his fingers. They were clean.

- The field of operation has changed, he said slowly. - Whoever brings a human to action first rewrites the Thread. It cannot be changed a second time.

Pak turned to Dream:

- We will have to go to the people. Race her.

And for the first time in all that time Dream said nothing reassuring.
