The smell of ozone still sat in his throat, and the clumsy tall man's words had lodged somewhere at the base of his skull, vibrating with that same violet ring. Elias gulped air, backing out of the stifling dark of the courtyard into the blazing hall of the San Francisco gallery. He was finishing his champagne, smiling at guests, listening to Dream whisper in his ear as always: "Return to the blueprints. There is peace there" — and he did not yet know this night would be the last.

They came when the spotlights went out.

Shiny was not going to waste words on persuasion. Why speak to someone who had locked himself in a cage of correct lines and fear of mediocrity? Easier to burn the cage itself.

Her Brother moved through the dark gallery silently and methodically, like a professional liquidator. From the plastic canister in his hands, gasoline thudded onto the parquet and Elias's flawless blueprints. Shiny stood in the center of the hall, her huge, frighteningly still eyes catching the pale light of the streetlamps. An expensive lighter turned lazily between her fingers; a satisfied smile played on her face.

"Now he'll have to start over," she said.

One click — and fire devoured everything. The walls, the "golden boy" awards, his perfect building models, and those same lifeless, ugly paintings in the dark corner of the courtyard. Shiny watched the roaring flame with the delight of a mad creator. She truly believed she was performing an act of mercy. She was giving his Thirst its freedom, destroying everything that kept him from becoming a new da Vinci. From this ash he was meant to rise an artist who would own the minds of millions.

Her Brother stood beside her. The pale flicker of flame slid across his face, but there was no delight in his eyes.

"Are you sure this is what he wanted?"

Shiny spun toward him.

"He wanted to burn. You saw it yourself. Those paintings were a cage, architecture was a cage, his whole tidy life was a cage. I only destroyed the walls! Let him understand that all of this can be lost and you don't die. Let him finally paint what he's afraid of."

That night in the Garden, the Thread of the "Great Architect" snapped with such a deafening crack that Elias, watching the fire from across the street, nearly bled from the ears.

But the Thread veered sharply aside. Elias watched his exhibition burn, his architectural name, his hidden paintings, his last beautiful attempt to become someone great and understandable. Fire consumed everything, yet nothing inside him tore back toward them. Elias finally understood where his true hunger lay. He did not need brushes. He exhaled with relief.

One Year Later

Shiny and her Brother sat in an expensive, half-lit bar. On the large screen above the counter, an emergency headline flashed: "Madness on K2: solo winter ascent without oxygen."

On the grainy photo — Elias. Frostbitten, exhausted, owning nothing anymore except that icy summit. A true dead man who was more alive than anyone living.

Shiny, furious, slammed her expensive phone onto the marble table with a whistle so sharp the cocktail in her glass splashed the polished surface.

"I burned everything for him! I gave him a chance to shine, destroyed his suffocating past! I thought he'd start writing masterpieces, overturn art! And he… just climbed a piece of ice where no one can see him. Turned his life to ash for one minute in the cold. He failed!"

Her Brother slowly set his glass of neat bourbon back on the marble. His gaze, solid and heavy as bedrock, was fixed on the screen where Elias appeared a tiny but unbearably bright, pulsing point.

"No, sister. The paintings and the offices were the same cages for him. You burned one, and he understood he no longer had to pretend to be a painter. His true Thirst was for this — for physical victory over death."

He turned to Shiny, and the cold reflection of a distant peak showed in his eyes:

"Look at him. Up there, he owns the whole world right now. He didn't trade architecture for miserable scribbles — he traded all your cheap smoke for true Greatness. Now he shines brighter than all your diamonds, Shiny. It's just that this light… isn't for sale."
