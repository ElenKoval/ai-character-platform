Elias retreated into a corner, away from the gallery’s blinding spotlights. He gripped his champagne glass so hard it might have been a life ring. He desperately believed these paintings were his salvation. His true path.

— Heh-heh… Elias. Why are you making so much noise? Your thoughts smell like burnt wiring right now. Mmm.

The voice came from deep shadow behind him. Dry, thin, like the rustle of bark splitting.

Elias flinched and spun around. In the courtyard dark, pressed to a damp wall, sat a gangly, long boy. He looked strangely fragile and angular, as if his bones had been joined wrong, and he rocked in some private, invisible rhythm.

— Who’s there?! I… I’m just looking at my work. It’s quiet here. Here I’m finally real.

— Real? No, kid. Right now you’re just a piece of an overtightened string. Wait… Do you hear that purple ringing? The back of your head vibrates so hard my spores are itching. Dream thinks you’ll go back to blueprints. Shiny thinks he’ll light the flame of art in you. They look at you from above, Elias. I look from below, from under the roots. And I see what they can’t. It’s not tight in this room. It’s tight inside your skull.

The long boy pressed thin fingers to his own temples, as if someone else’s tension were splitting him apart.

— You don’t understand! — Elias jabbed at the canvases. — My projects, my perfect buildings — all of it was a mistake. This is my only path! I’ll live for this!

— Path? Heh… Paintings are just another coat of plaster you’re using to patch a breach. You want something so badly the air around you is cracking. I feel that drop-off under your feet with my skin. The ground under your “life plan” washed away long ago, Elias. You’re stretched so tight you’re about to snap. You’re one big crack, and something truly wild is seeping through. Do you hear it? The soil of the Evil Forest is already tasting your Thirst. No paint there, no offices. Cold, stone, thin air, and a silence where a human hears for the first time how much life is left in them.

He slid down the wall with unnatural ease and melted into the corner’s dark, as if he had crumbled into dry mycelium, leaving ozone in the air and a sudden, torn emptiness.

Elias gasped, snatching at the air with his mouth, as if all the oxygen in the gallery had at once retreated to an unreachable, deadly height.
