Weaver stood at the edge of the bed. Her human body seemed too fragile for the abyss pulsing inside her. She watched the sleeping man and woman with quiet, almost childlike triumph.

Suddenly her gaze changed. Without turning, she sighed:

- Cat..

A young man stepped out of the dense shadow in the corner. He came close, too close. The air between them thickened with familiar tension. The one who was meant to stop her, and the one for whom he was ready to break any order.

- You always feel me, Weaver. Even when I'm behind you.

- Especially when you're behind me. Did you come to take him?

She turned. Their eyes met. Hers feverish and burning with hope. His heavy, full of unspoken sorrow.

Weaver's fingers twitched and almost touched his jacket. Cat froze. In his eyes, possibilities flickered and died one by one.

- He could have become a great composer, Cat said quietly, looking at her, not at the man. - His music would have inspired millions.

Weaver stepped closer, closing the last inches between them.

- But now he's happy, she hissed near his lips. - He lives. He feels. He no longer has to die for sound.

She touched his shoulder. In that touch there was as much love as defiance.

Cat closed his eyes for a moment.

- He lives, he repeated softly. - But the world went deaf. You saved a man, Weaver. And killed the god inside him.

A lock clicked. The tension between Cat and Weaver froze under the cold brought in by Pak entering.

Frost crept along the walls at his presence. The space tightened.

Weaver hissed, sharp and low. She looked at Cat one last time. The look was long, promising, desperate. Then she dissolved sharply into the air.

Pak stopped by the bed. His cold gaze scanned the sleeping man's Threads. He raised his hand, trying to return them to their former, higher trajectory. His fingers slid across the thick golden glow Weaver had woven into reality.

He slowly lowered his hand.

- We were too late.

The sleeper's face remained still, but inside it shone the light of gentle human warmth.

- The world is poorer. For a smile. An irrational choice.

Cat stood in silence. He could still feel Weaver's touch on his shoulder.

There was pain in his eyes, and Pak saw it - not because of the lost composer.

And said nothing.
