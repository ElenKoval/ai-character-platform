Weaver tore away. She did not know exactly what she was running from. Somewhere inside she understood she was running from Cat's strange gaze and from that strange stillness that had seized the world for an instant when their eyes met. Damn. And yet everything had been so simple: a strange world, a strange her, calm Dream who could not answer her questions but at least tried. Simple laughter, the simple speed of her run, the quiet rustle of Threads. But this was different. She did not know the word, but she knew for certain it was not simple. It was something deep. Damn, is there really something else inside? Why?

So deep that for a moment Weaver stopped understanding where her body ended and the world began. Her chest grew tight, and heat rose beneath her chitin. Something inside stretched toward a place where it no longer was, and from that she wanted to scream, bite, laugh, and tear the air with her limbs all at once. Speed had always been simple, so Weaver kept racing forward without understanding where she was running or why. Simply toward a place with no foreign eyes. In truth she was only afraid of running into Dream - he would ask what had happened to her. And she would have to explain something for which she had no words yet.

Weaver felt the smooth, familiar surface vanish beneath her thin limbs. The world turned uneven and damp, which was impossible - there could be no rain here. Weaver stopped. Before her stood the Evil Forest. It did not look like the forests of the human world Dream had shown her, but it did not look like the Garden of Threads either.

Weaver braked so sharply her limbs slid on the black roots. Something stirred in the depths of the thicket.

- Little one, the Forest rustled.

Weaver stepped back.

- I am not little.

The Forest laughed quietly. Laughter ran along the branches with a dry crackle.

- Of course not. Little ones do not come here with such heat inside.

She froze.

- What is it? Do you know what is inside me?

- You know yourself.

- No! I do not want this!

- Not yet.

Weaver wanted to turn and run back, but something flared among the roots. A black, heavy Shadow rose - very distantly resembling a Thread, yet horribly emptying. It was clear that it hurt. Weaver realized with horror that many such shadows still lay between the roots, pulsing, twitching like living pieces of someone else's pain. Some were almost dead; others still stretched somewhere, as if searching for the place they had been torn from.

Weaver stared at them without blinking.

- What is this? she asked more quietly.

The Forest stirred, but slowly and wearily, as if deciding whether to answer at all.

- That which was not allowed to remain.

One of the Shadows trembled, and Weaver felt it with her whole body.

Damn! In the Shadow was the same tightness, the same heat, the same impossible pull toward something that was not nearby but without which everything inside suddenly grew too empty. She took a step back.

- Are they sick?

- No.

- Dead?

- Worse. - The Forest leaned lower, and his branches creaked above her. - They endure.

Weaver jerked her head up.

- Why?

The Forest was silent. And in that silence Weaver heard the answer - vast, ancient, black, the answer of another's patience. She grew afraid, because she understood: this was her answer too.

- I am leaving, she said.

- Of course, the Forest rustled.

She turned.

- Do not call me. I will not come again. I need to find Dream.

- I did not call you.

Weaver stopped.

- Then why did I come?

The Forest was silent for a long time, but at last answered, almost tenderly:

- Because you heard what the others learned not to hear.

Weaver did not answer. She broke into a run back toward the Garden so fast it seemed she could leave behind the Forest, the Shadows, and that gaze after which too much room for pain had opened inside her. But the Forest already knew Weaver would return. At least he hoped so very much.
