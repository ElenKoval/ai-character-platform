Pak and Cat's friendship was a strange one.

They were both quiet, but a flame lived inside each of them. Only Pak's was cold: thin, white, cutting the air like a scalpel. Cat's was restless, searching, darting between worlds, rooftops, other people's windows, and his own questions that no one was going to answer.

- Pak, Cat asked one day, sitting on the edge of a stone slab in the Garden. - You always see the Thirst before anyone else. I think Dream even envies you. How do you do it?

Pak did not answer at once. He was studying one of the Threads, frowning as if its very shape were a personal insult.

- Simple. When the structure breaks, it infuriates me.

Cat blinked.

- That's it? I'm supposed to be satisfied with an answer like that?

- Things that infuriate me, I see at once.

Cat snorted softly, but the smile vanished quickly.

- So I'm completely useless, then.

Pak turned his head toward him.

- An interesting opening for another foolishness.

- No, really. Dream sees the Thread's path, you handle the Thirst coldly and precisely, Liora heals with warmth. Crystal even manages to tune a Thread like an instrument. What do I do? Why am I here?

Pak was silent.

- Weaver does nothing at all.

- I've never seen her. Why?

Pak frowned.

- You should roam human rooftops less.

Cat wanted to answer, but did not have time.

A ripple passed through the space.

It was strange. In the Dryad's world there was no real air, yet something around them suddenly trembled, as if an invisible surface of water. The ripple touched Pak, wrapped around him, slowed him.

For an instant everything turned viscous and transparent, like resin. And Pak saw it all from the side - Cat standing motionless, and Weaver.

Weaver tore through the Garden at an impossible speed - small, swift, alive to the point of madness. She flashed past them so fast that an ordinary gaze could not even hold her outline.

But Pak saw Weaver, and how the world stopped for two.

For Cat and for Weaver.

Cat stood motionless. Quiet, stunned, as if an entire ocean of ice water had been poured over him.

Weaver turned her head for a fraction of a moment, and her eyes flared.

As if heat had struck her from within - not fear, not joy, but something unusual, for which she had no word - a kind of recognition of something that did not yet have a name.

Pak exhaled slowly.

- Fuck.

The resin vanished; reality snapped back as if nothing had happened.

Weaver was already gone, but Cat still stood in place, impossibly quiet, unbearably quiet.

Pak looked at him and understood: something had happened that would change everything beyond repair.

- Is that… Cat swallowed. His voice sounded as if he were asking for help without understanding what he needed saving from. - Is that Weaver?

- Yes.

- Why?

Pak narrowed his eyes.

- Why what?

Cat looked, confused, to where she had vanished.

- I… don't know. Why I…

He fell silent.

Pak looked at him for a long time.

Pak slowly turned back to his Thread, but his fingers froze in the air for a moment. He knew the physics of this world inside out, yet for the first time he did not understand how to mend what had broken inside the boy in a single second.

And Cat stood there, feeling how beneath his ribs, at the very bottom of his searching, restless flame, the first real, deaf knot of his life was slowly tightening.
