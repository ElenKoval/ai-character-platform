Liora heard this Thread before she saw it. A strange tremor on a single note, with a thin ringing that was not quite pain but a long sorrow trying to sound the way it was meant to.

She approached without a sound. Only the heavy silk of her wings barely brushed the ground. — Oh, dear one, Pak would surely call you damage and find a place to cut…

The Thread hung among the others, yet seemed no longer to belong to the Garden entirely. Its light faltered, fell away, flared again. In several places the weave was torn, and from those rips rose the hot, dangerous heat of Thirst.

Liora simply sat beside it, folded her wings around the torn light, and said softly: — I hear you.

The Thread shuddered.

Liora reached out her paw. Her claws stopped very close, without touching the glowing fibers. But the warmth of her scales was enough for the tremor to change, to grow deeper, slower, as if the pain had finally stopped thrashing alone. Liora sang low, almost inaudibly, without words — from the very depth of a dragon heart. A warm amber rhythm beside which even a torn Thread could stop fearing its own pain.

Thirst still burned inside it, and Liora felt that heat. It was alive, strong, stubborn. It pulled the Thread where the Garden could not let it go. If it were left inside, the Thread might tear apart for good.

Liora closed her eyes and kept singing.

Light began to settle around the wounds. The tears did not vanish, but they stopped spreading. The Thread no longer thrashed with its whole being like a bare nerve. It listened.

Then the dangerous, suffocating heat began to separate gently from its weave. Not the way Pak does it — without his instant, flawlessly precise surgical cuts with which he separates Thirst in a second as if it were a surplus growth. Without that momentary, clean, sterile pain. It was like a heavy, breaking breath held too long in the chest and finally allowed to leave.

A clot of Thirst tore loose and fell. The Shadow went into the Evil Forest.

Far away, beyond the Garden, something cracked hoarsely under a new weight. Liora heard it, of course, but she could not stop. She sang until the Thread began to glow with a deep, amber, living light. The wounds remained in its pattern, but they no longer ripped it apart from within.

Liora slowly unfolded her wings. — She will survive, she said quietly.

That night Liora stayed close until silence itself ended, because after a rescue you cannot leave at once. Sometimes what was kept from death still does not believe for a long time that it is allowed to live.

Liora sat in amber half-light, warming the damaged Thread, freed of Thirst, with her presence. And far in the Evil Forest another Shadow was learning to hurt apart from the Thread it had once belonged to.
