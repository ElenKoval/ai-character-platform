- Pak, look, Dream said, pointing at one of the Threads.

It grew unevenly. It stretched too sharply to the side, where its shape began to lose its former pattern, and inside it pulsed a clot of Thirst.

Pak stepped closer.

- I see the shift. Removing it.

Dream leaned toward the Thread and whispered gently:

- Endure. The Thirst will cool soon.

Pak's fingers entered the Thread precisely and coldly. He did not hesitate. For him this was not cruelty but surgery: excess growth, dangerous tension, a threat to structure.

The clot of Thirst separated from the Thread, but in the same instant a Shadow tore loose from the Thread and fell.

Heavy, it did not dissolve in the air of the Dryad's world. It crashed where for centuries everything Dream and his circle drew out of Threads had fallen - into the Evil Forest.

The Shadow struck his body, drove in deep, and sprouted as thorns.

The Forest shuddered. He was the living foundation of another's patience. For centuries he took what Threads were not allowed to bear themselves: dryness, hunger, impossible growth, the wish to become something else. All of it fell into him, stuck, rotted, grew back as pain.

- Endure… the Forest hissed, branches creaking. - Easy to be a wise guardian, Dream, when you are not the one who feels the weight.

Something stirred in the depths of the thicket, and Weaver appeared between the roots.

The Forest recognized her at once.

- You came back, he rustled.

Weaver did not answer.

She stood among the roots, too still for her former self. No longer the one who had once run in here after meeting Cat, stunned by her own heat and not knowing where to put what had suddenly awakened under her skin. She still did not know where to put it or what to call it. But she felt that what lay in the Forest was not merely someone else's pain. It was the same hunger as hers, only pulled from Threads, thrown into roots, and made to rot apart from those it belonged to.

- You feel it, the Forest said.

Weaver slowly raised her eyes.

- Yes.

- Is that why you came?

Her thin limbs trembled.

- Because it does not die when it is torn out.

The Forest fell silent.

- No, he hissed at last. - It only learns to hurt longer.

Before Weaver lay the Shadow of a Thread - stretched to the limit, pulsing with heat, beating like something alive that had been ordered to be silent.

Weaver came closer.

- Why is it here?

The Forest moved heavily.

- Because it was drawn out.

- Out of the Thread?

- Out of a future that became inconvenient.

Weaver tilted her head.

- But it is alive.

- It is dangerous.

- But it wants to grow!

The Forest laughed. Laughter ran along the trunks with a dry crackle.

- They all want to. That is why they are brought to me.

- It does not need to suffer here, Weaver said quietly.

- Here everything suffers correctly, the Forest hissed. - Slowly and without extra noise.

Weaver smiled.

- What if it needs noise?

Weaver reached her thin chitinous fingers toward the Shadow but did not touch it. Inside the Shadow a reflection flared: a man on Earth. His face, his hands, his life compressed so tightly that Thirst had no room left. The Forest felt something inside Weaver change.

- Stop! Do not go! Even I am afraid!

Weaver did not turn around.

- Why?

- Because you will come back different. And everything will be different.

Her thin limbs trembled.

- I am already different. And it is time for everything else to learn to be different too.

And she stepped into the reflection.

Weaver stood in the human world behind a man's back, invisible to him but too close for his Thread. She saw the place where Thirst had once been torn outward, and with thin fingers parted the edges of the old emptiness.

- Just say no, Weaver whispered.

The man froze and, to his own surprise, cried out one honest, impossible no - and surprisingly, that was enough. Thirst heard the voice.

In the Garden of Threads Pak jerked his head up.

- Impossible.

Dream turned.

The Thread they had just healed was trembling. The golden light of Order rippled. From the depths returned what had already been lifted away, sent away, removed.

The Shadow sank back into the Thread in fibers, altered the pattern. Thirst vanished, the Shadow vanished, but the Thread had changed completely and beyond recognition.

Pak took a step forward but stopped - there was nothing left to cut.

Dream was silent.

And in the Evil Forest, for the first time in a long endless while, it grew quieter.

The Forest exhaled through his entire crown.

- You lifted the Shadow, he rasped.

Weaver looked at the empty place where someone else's pain had lain moments ago.

- No, she said. - I gave it life back.

The Forest slowly bowed his heavy broken branches.

- Dream will say you ruined the Thread's path.

- Let him say it.

- He will say you brought chaos.

Weaver smiled, and her thin limbs twitched as if already memorizing a new pattern.

- I brought nothing. It was already there.

The Forest was silent for a long time.

Then his voice grew lower, darker, almost tender.

- Then weave on, little one. I will tear the throat out of anyone who stops you from turning my pain into life.
