Before the First Cry

Even before time existed in the world of the Great Dryad, there were already those who did not remember their birth.

The Dryad was first.

She did not rule this world or give orders. She was its beginning, its motionless heart. Then she sank into an eternal sleep of silence, fused with a giant tree that from afar alone could be mistaken for a tree. Beside her remained Kiper, the faithful Kiper, the one who did not sleep, the one who had watched too long.

Perhaps he remembers everything: the first decisions, the first fractures, the first mistakes of this world. But memory grew too heavy for him. It no longer formed straight speech. Sometimes Kiper pulled truth from it, but more often a distorted fragment, and even more often delirium in which something real could still be felt.

No creature could understand him fully anymore.

Only Dream tried. Dream remembered the beginning - the instant from which the universe was later born. But everything between that first instant and the appearance of the first human was erased. When Dream tried to remember, memory slipped away like water through his fingers.

His stable memory began with the first Thread.

No one knows who made the Threads. They simply appeared. Dream remembers the first appearing, and then there were so many that the world of the Great Dryad could no longer be imagined without their eternal whisper.

Each Thread was a reflection of a person on Earth. But a Thread was not fate at all. It did not show who a person would become, whom they would love, how long they would live, or how they would die. A Thread simply grew.

Why Dream knew how a Thread should grow - only Kiper could know that, but he could hardly have told anyone.

Dream served the Threads. When there were too many Threads, other beings joined Dream, as strange as he and as forgetful of their past: Pak, Liora, Crystal, and Cat.

Together with Dream they watched Threads stretch, tangle, bend, sometimes try to become what they were not meant to be - then Thirst rose inside, dangerous, hot, able to change growth beyond recognition. And then they intervened.

Pak saw structural violation before anyone else and acted as a surgeon: precisely, coldly, without pity for what he considered pathology.

Liora did not cut. She warmed damaged Threads with voice, song, and amber heat until Thirst withdrew.

Crystal tuned Threads like instruments. Where tension could tear them, he sought a sound through which pain could leave not as a wound but as music.

Cat appeared later than the rest - almost an adolescent, too alive for the motionless Garden.

So the Garden worked: Thirst rose in Threads, Dream and his circle returned a Thread to its path.

But Thirst did not vanish. It fell where for centuries everything Threads were not allowed to bear themselves had fallen - into the Evil Forest, which took another's thirst, drove it in as Shadows, sprouted thorns, and left it to hurt.

Dream believed he saved Threads, and perhaps he did. But the Forest suffered for them.

Beyond the Garden was another place - the Wasteland.

No one lived there, though there were more Threads than anywhere else. From afar they merged into one gray tangled mass.

The Wasteland was more terrible than death, which at least ends, and more terrible than evil, which at least has a purpose, more terrible than pain, which at least is a feeling.

In the Wasteland there was nothing - only empty Threads.

They belonged to people who did not break, did not scream, did not reach for light and did not ask for darkness. They simply existed. Their reflections on Earth could speak, work, build houses, bear children, buy things, laugh in the right places, and die on the appointed day, but their Threads remained lines without inner movement.

Dream knew of the Wasteland and simply did not want to look there.

In the Garden there was always something to save: growth, error, pain, a dangerous bend, Thirst. In the Wasteland nothing could be returned to a path, because there was no path there either.

The world of the Great Dryad was a world of stagnation and order. Time did not exist here. Nothing aged, hurried, or ended. Nothing changed except the Threads.

So it continued until the first cry tore through the silence of the roots where Dream found her. The first being to appear in the Dryad's world as a child. A small strange creature with chitinous limbs, too fast, too alive, impossible for a world where no one had ever seen childhood. And Weaver grew - which was already a terrible violation, because everyone else appeared fully formed. It is no surprise that Weaver did what no one in the Dryad's world had done yet: she did not let Thirst rot in the roots of the Evil Forest, but turned another's pain into a small, almost unnoticed, yet real human step. She gave Thirst a way to return to the Thread with a new pattern, new length, and new possibility of growth - changing it forever. From that moment the struggle moved beyond the Garden. Now the rules were not set by the Garden, the Forest, or Dream, but by the first human act.
